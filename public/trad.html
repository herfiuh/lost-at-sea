Then

Mariners used landmarks, currents, seabirds, water colour, reefs and other environmental signs to understand where they were.

Near shore, this could mean the difference between reaching land safely and running aground.

Today

GPS, charts, sonar and satellites give us precise information.

But if technology fails, the environment is still giving you information.

💡 The survival question

“If all my electronics stopped working, what could I learn by simply looking around?”

Nature doesn't give you coordinates. It gives you clues.

🐦 1. Seabird Flight Trajectory

Birds can reveal the direction of nearby land.

In the simulation: seabirds flying WEST toward their roost indicate land lies WEST.
STATION RULE: Birds flying WEST → Qatari mainland / shoreline lies WEST.

🪸 2. Fasht Reef Avoidance

Water colour can reveal a dangerous change in depth.

EMERALD

SAFE CHANNEL

10–20 m

DARK PATCH

DANGER

Fasht reef < 3 m

STATION RULE: Dark patches → possible Fasht reef → turn OUTWARD toward deeper water immediately.
