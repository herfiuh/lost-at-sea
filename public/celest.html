Then

Before GPS, sailors crossed open water by reading the natural world. The Sun provided directional clues; stars could help determine position.

Gulf and Indian Ocean sailors developed detailed traditions for navigating long distances without modern electronics.

Today

Ships use GPS, electronic charts, radar and digital sensors. But the basic process remains:

CELESTIAL DIVISION 01 / 04
Celestial Training

☀️ + ⭐ Read the Sky

☀️ 1. Solar Shadows

A shadow points AWAY from its light source.

The shadow extends away from the Sun.
STATION RULE:
NE shadow → Sun is SW → keep the NE shadow over your LEFT SHOULDER → SOUTH

⭐ 2. Polaris & Latitude

Polaris's altitude ≈ your latitude.

The angle above the horizon gives an approximate latitude.

OUR START

29.20° N

OUR TARGET

25.17° N

29.20° N   →   27.00° N   →   25.17° N
↓ LOWER LATITUDE = SOUTH ↓
STATION RULE: If Polaris is higher than 25.17°, keep steering SOUTH.

☀️ Challenge 1 — Shadow

SHADOW → ↗ NE

Ask:

“Where is the Sun?”
“What does our steering rule tell us?”

Students should discover: SW Sun → SOUTH.

⭐ Challenge 2 — Polaris

POLARIS = 28° TARGET = 25.17°

Ask:

“Are we north or south of the target?”
“Which way do we need to go?”

Students should discover: 28° is still north of 25.17° → SOUTH.

🧭 The real-world question

“If you were actually stranded somewhere with no GPS, could these clues help you?”

Let them discuss it. The point isn't that two observations magically give perfect navigation. The point is that nature still provides usable information.

✅ Ready for the mission?

Your students are ready when they can:

  • Explain the shadow/Sun relationship.
  • Explain what Polaris's angle tells us.
  • Use 25.17° as the target latitude.
  • Explain their decision using evidence.
